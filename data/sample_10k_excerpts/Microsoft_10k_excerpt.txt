MICROSOFT CORPORATION -- Form 10-K (excerpt, public domain)

Item 1A. Risk Factors

Cybersecurity, Data Privacy, and Platform Abuses
Threats to information technology security take a variety of forms. Threat
actors -- including sophisticated nation-state and nation-state-supported
actors -- continually engage in attacks against our networks, products and
services. Successful breaches could result in disclosure of confidential
information, regulatory fines, loss of customer trust, and harm to our
reputation. We invest substantial resources in cybersecurity controls,
detection, and response capabilities, but cannot guarantee these will be
sufficient.

Artificial Intelligence
Issues related to AI may give rise to legal, regulatory, ethical, and
reputational risks. We may face increased regulation of AI products,
challenges to training data sourcing, claims of IP infringement, and rising
operating costs for compute capacity. Our AI products may produce inaccurate
outputs that can lead to harms to users and to the Company.

Competition
We face strong competition across each of our business segments, including
from open-source software, large established companies, and a long tail of
startups. Open-source AI models and cloud-native competitors are eroding
historical moats. Our cloud business in particular faces well-funded
hyperscale competitors.

Foreign Operations
We conduct significant business in many foreign jurisdictions. Foreign
exchange rate fluctuations can negatively impact our revenues and earnings.
Geopolitical tensions, including those between the United States and other
nations, may impair our ability to operate freely in some markets.

Share Repurchases and Dividend
We return cash to shareholders through ongoing share buybacks and a
quarterly dividend. The amount of buybacks varies with our cash position
and strategic priorities.
